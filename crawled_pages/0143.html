Remembering Eugene Bardach

The world of public policy lost one of its towering intellectual figures with the passing of Professor Eugene Bardach, a beloved faculty member of the Goldman School of Public Policy. For more than five decades, Bardach shaped the field of policy analysis with a rare combination of intellectual rigor, practical wisdom, and genuine compassion for everyday people. 

Eugene Bardach was a political scientist with interests ranging from health policy and human services to environmental enforcement, fire prevention, and habitat preservation. In an era of relentless academic specialization, he was curious about what worked and what didn't work across every policy domain, driven always by one overarching question: How can we make government work better for ordinary citizens?

That question animated a lifetime of serious, rigorous, and deeply humanistic scholarship. 
"Gene Bardach was one of the true giants of public policy education, and his loss is felt deeply by everyone at the Goldman School," said Dean David C. Wilson. “For more than five decades, he shaped not only this institution, but an entire field. He leaves behind a legacy that will guide and inspire this School for generations to come."

Bardach published five major works and is best known for A Practical Guide for Policy Analysis: The Eightfold Path to More Effective Problem Solving. The book is now in its seventh edition and is coauthored by his former student and Brown University Professor Eric M. Patashnik. The book now has a global reach, having been translated into more than seven languages.

The Eightfold Path teaches students and practitioners to analyze policy by moving through eight essential steps: defining problems, assembling evidence, constructing alternatives, selecting criteria, projecting outcomes, confronting tradeoffs, making decisions, and telling their story to an audience. 

"It is a systematic guide to clear thinking about the most complex challenges we face," said Professor Patashnik, who delivered a moving eulogy in tribute to his mentor and coauthor. "It is an optimistic and generous book, one that shows readers not that progress is easy, let alone guaranteed, but that with honest, critical thinking, it is possible."

A Teacher Who Changed Lives

Beginning in 1973, Bardach co-taught the Introduction to Policy Analysis and Advanced Policy Analysis workshops at GSPP. Over the course of his career, he coached more than five hundred policy analysis projects in which students analyzed real issues for real-world clients in the public, nonprofit, and private sectors.

"I remember going to Gene and Nancy’s home every week for our APA seminar," recalls Anne Campbell Washington (MPP ‘00), who serves as Senior Assistant Dean of Academic Programs and Dean of Students at GSPP. "We would eat dinner together, and Gene would lead us in these incredible conversations about policy analysis. All of us in that group felt like we had won the lottery in getting to work with him. It was a true highlight of my GSPP education."

"Gene Bardach and I were friends and colleagues for 53 years, starting in Fall 1973, the year before I joined the GSPP faculty," said Professor Emeritus Lee Friedman. "He came to visit me at Yale, and we talked excitedly for hours about how to use professional knowledge to improve public policy. He told me that GSPP was a new school trying to train people to do just that. He had read every author I brought up in our conversation, sharing my enthusiasm and adding critical new questions about each work we discussed. I joined the School knowing I had found an incredible intellectual comrade, a feeling that only deepened over the many years that we had together."

"Gene brought craft, social science knowledge, common sense, and a commitment to his family and GSPP together in his work in a way that coalesced into deep wisdom about the world," said Professor and former GSPP dean Henry E. Brady. "His good humor, friendly questioning, and helpful probing reflected his endless curiosity about everything, his deep commitment to fairness and human decency, and his desire to make the world a better place. As my colleague for almost fifty years, Gene provided friendship, guidance, support, and a model for what public policy was all about. Nobody did it better."

Professor Bardach with Professor Hilary Hoynes in 2018 when they were both inducted into the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

 

Recognition and Legacy

Eugene Bardach's contributions to his field were widely recognized during his lifetime. He served as president of the Association for Policy Analysis and Management, received prestigious scholarly awards, and was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

Gifts in honor of Eugene Bardach can be made to the Eugene and Nancy Bardach Graduate Student Support Fund.
